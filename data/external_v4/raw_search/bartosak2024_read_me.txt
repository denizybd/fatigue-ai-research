The associated file "316L_fatigue_and_defects.xlsx" contains data utilized in Section 4 of the article. The dataset contains data of 20 specimens of additively manufactured AISI 316L stainless steel, on which the geometries of defects were analyzed and low-cycle fatigue tests were performed. Specific details on the geometry, manufacturing, x-ray tomography and fatigue tests are given in the article.

The first sheet named "Fatigue" contains the data relevant to the low-cycle fatigue tests. The first column "ID" are simply numbers to identify each specimen. The second column "Pswt [MPa]" is the Smith-Watson-Topper (SWT) parameter, which summarizes the whole loading cycle into a single damage parameter. The SWT parameter is defined as the product of maximum normal stress and the normal strain amplitude on the critical plane. For this dataset, the critical plane was defined as the plane experiencing maximum tensile strain. Next, the column "Nf [-]" lists the number of cycles to failure, which is defined by a point where the the amplitude of the equivalent stress decreases by 10% from the tangent line though the saturated part of the evolution of these amplitudes with cycles. Finally, the last column "Loading path" indicates the shape of the strain loading path. The axial and torsional paths are self-explanatory. The proportional path means the axial and shear strains were in-phase and the ratio of shear/axial strains is equal to the square root of 3.

The remaining sheets named "Specimen_{ID}" contain the features of defects in each specimen. The first column "Projected area [mm2]" lists the area projected by the defect onto the critical plane, which is defined above. The table is sorted according to this characteristic in descending order and contains 10 defects with largest projected areas. The second column "Sphericity [-]" is a feature representing the shape of the defect and it is defined as the ratio of the surface area of a sphere with the volume of the defect to the actual surface area of the defect. Next, the column "Distance from free surface [mm]" denotes the distance from the center of a circumscribed sphere of the defect to the free surface of the specimen. Finally, the column "Defect diameter [mm]" denotes the diameters of these spheres as an alternative feature describing size of the defects.
