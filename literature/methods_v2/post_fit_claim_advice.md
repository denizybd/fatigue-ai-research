## Strong-control diagnostic

The initial unpooled control used pooled total scatter, confounding prior borrowing with a different slope/scatter model. The explicit post-result diagnostic holds the hierarchy population slope and within-condition sigma fixed, removes only the target prior and includes observed-curvature intercept uncertainty. Reconstructed original hierarchy predictions agree within1e-9; no population was refitted.

On matched available internalA k3query pairs, hierarchy versus this control gives1.10times lower life, with95percent interval0.96-1.35times; no dominance is established. Pair availability is98.15percent because all-runout support leaves the unpenalized intercept unidentified. Hierarchy retains100percent availability by its proper prior. Risk on the matched subset is7.78-8.52percent for hierarchy versus8.44-9.03percent for the control; the paired identified risk-change interval spans zero.

On the external random panel at k3, the same-population control has6.99percent violation compared with hierarchy13.19percent, returning lower life limits. This is a risk/utility tradeoff, not evidence that hierarchical target-prior borrowing is necessary or superior. All external groups remain one campaign, so no between-campaign confidence interval is warranted.

## Contribution assessment

We have a stronger source-audited benchmark and counterexample study, not a new dependable-design algorithm. The strongest story combines corrected data semantics, explicit finite-source calibration limits, few-shot adaptation gains and their failure under a fresh campaign/order shift, and a matched control that prevents attributing ordinary target-intercept updating to an advanced prior. The positive part is operationally meaningful but insufficiently precise to support a broad three-tests-guarantee claim.

Hierarchical small-sample fatigue modeling, transfer learning and conformal survival prediction are prior work. A publication-grade method contribution would require original campaign/build/specimen mapping, consistent endpoint definitions, a strong fatigue-limit or random-slope baseline, and several independently held experimental campaigns under a frozen acquisition rule. Further tuning of these already-inspected test sets cannot supply that confirmation.
