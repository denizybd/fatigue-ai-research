# Independent phase-2 contribution and claim review

Written 2026-10-20 after the frozen phase-2 outcomes. This is an interpretation audit of the saved protocols, summaries, paired source contrasts and source/seed metrics. No new fit, search or eligibility change was performed for this review.

## Judgment

The requested question has been tested substantially more credibly than in the first pilot. There is promising evidence that a few target endpoints can correct a poorly transferred fatigue curve. There is **not yet convincing evidence that three experiments give generally reliable, more useful lower-life bounds for a genuinely new manufacturing condition**. The prespecified calibrated primary claim fails because every available 90% endpoint-calibrated bound is zero. The secondary raw-model average looks encouraging, but its uncertainty, source heterogeneity and controls prevent a strong superiority or safety claim.

This is worth discussing with Tridello as a concrete provenance and transfer-validation problem, accompanied by a corrected benchmark and a focused collaboration/data request. It is not ready to be presented as a successful new trustworthy prediction method or as a refutation of the original Centola analysis. The useful contribution currently is the auditable separation of physical endpoint provenance, publication reuse, censoring ambiguity and genuinely held-out adaptation; methodological novelty remains to be established against the relevant physical and censor-aware baselines.

## What the frozen results actually establish

The four files in `protocols/v2/` preserve the original endpoint-calibrated primary criterion and label full-training raw, curvature and population-bootstrap tracks secondary. The small-source amendment was written before the fits, so adding the raw track is not a result-driven rescue, but it does not replace the failed primary criterion.

The final corpus has eleven provenance blocks available for historical fitting. Panel A evaluates nine eligible source blocks, not eleven independent target campaigns and not three times as many independent observations because three seeds are used. Here k=3 means three support endpoints per target curve/condition, not three total endpoints across every curve from a held-out source. `strict/run_receipt.json` has 4–6 calibration blocks while retaining 4–6 fitting blocks. The finite rank needed for 90% endpoint calibration requires at least nine calibration blocks. Accordingly all calibrated bounds are zero, their dangerous-overprediction rate is zero by construction, and their log-life gain is undefined. Calling this “safe prediction succeeds” would conceal the complete loss of utility. With a held-out target and a minimum four fitting blocks, fourteen independent eligible blocks is the arithmetic minimum for nine calibration blocks; fourteen DOI labels alone would not establish that independence or transportability.

For secondary full-training raw hierarchical Basquin in panel A, `fulltraining/summary.csv` gives known-to-possible dangerous-overprediction risk 13.35–15.41% at k=0 and 8.31–8.93% at k=3. These are **censoring identification endpoints**, not confidence limits. Paired mean log10 lower-life gain is 0.2841, or 1.924×. Its descriptive 95% source-bootstrap interval is −0.07398 to 0.56275 decades, equivalently 0.843–3.654×. It neither excludes no gain nor establishes the prespecified gain greater than 1.25×. The paired identified true risk-change interval is −7.10 to −5.04 percentage points on the observed source mixture, but the upper 95% descriptive bootstrap endpoint for its upper identification endpoint is +9.30 points. This distinction matters: complete sign identification conditional on these observed endpoints is not convincing evidence of risk reduction for new source populations. The corresponding bootstrap endpoint for the difference of risk-upper endpoints is +8.29 points, also above the +2-point target. These intervals are descriptive because folds share training data.

The source average conceals a material failure mode. At k=3 Lai has 37.04–42.60% risk (versus 13.89–23.33% at k=0), Wood remains at 15.38%, and Kędziora moves from 0 to 11.11%. Spierings moves from 0 to 6.94% and Yu from 0 to 1.28%. Solberg moves from 90.91–100% to 3.03%; that single source drives the pooled safety improvement. An arithmetic diagnostic using the saved source metrics, with no refitting, gives a +4.83-point mean risk-upper change across the other eight sources. This is not a replacement estimate selected after seeing results; it explains why the overall negative contrast is fragile. Lai k3 risk-upper spans 24.58–62.23% across the three frozen support seeds. Report source/seed heterogeneity alongside the average.

The pooled baseline correctly stays unchanged with support. The unpooled intercept-shift control at k3 has about 2.12% observed risk and lower average lower-life predictions (mean log10 4.8087 versus hierarchical 5.2386), with 98.15% availability because all-runout support can make its unpenalized fit unavailable. Thus it is inaccurate to claim that the hierarchical model beats all strong controls: it trades greater lower-life utility against more observed risk. Any superiority claim needs a common prespecified safety level and paired queries including an explicit treatment of unavailable episodes. The hierarchical model retains proper censored support likelihood and shrinkage, but those sensible choices alone are not new methodological contributions.

Panels B and C are separate fixed-query populations, not additional points on panel A's learning curve. Full-training raw hierarchy at k3/k5 gives risk-upper 9.52/8.84% in B, but C gives 21.33/19.94/15.22% at k3/k5/k10. The larger support size does not establish a general monotone improvement. The straight/curved A summaries are very similar: k3 curved risk 8.69–9.31%, log gain 0.2691 decades with its interval crossing zero. Excluding the unresolved Afkhami stress-axis source leaves the same qualitative conclusion: k3 gain 0.2614 decades with interval −0.1083 to 0.5347, and upper bootstrap endpoint for upper identified true risk change +10.42 points. Neither sensitivity rescues the claim.

## External campaign: useful stress test, not independent replication of success

The external Trombley campaign contains 129 usable specimen records in three diameter groups. The groups form one related experimental campaign. At k=3 each of the three diameter groups contributes three support specimens, so evaluating all groups consumes nine support endpoints; k=10 uses thirty. Its crack/resonance-drop endpoint, ultrasonic testing, historical ordering and staircase allocation differ from full-fracture historical endpoints. Three diameter curves or three random support seeds must not be counted as three laboratories. Existing endpoints are retrospectively revealed; no physical experiment was run for this project.

In `external_fulltraining/summary.csv`, random-support raw hierarchical risk falls from 53.66% at k0 to 13.19% at k3. This is a large useful correction of an initially unsafe transferred curve, but remains above the 10% target. The log-life contrast is **−0.6705 decades**: k3 reduces the geometric predicted lower life to 21.4% of k0, about 4.68-fold lower. It is not an increase in usable design life. At k5 and k10 risk is 16.25% and 15.03%, respectively, so more endpoints do not remove the problem. Random k3 seed-specific risks span 3.04–22.33%.

Chronological supports reduce raw risk from 48.53% to 1.96% at k3, while lowering predicted life by 0.9456 decades, about 8.82-fold. This can be reported as the observed behavior of the frozen historical-date scenario. It cannot establish that choosing the earliest tests is a superior policy: random and chronological mechanisms reserve different support pools, so their held-query populations also differ. Both mechanisms were frozen beforehand, which protects against outcome-driven selection, but does not make their comparison causal or put it on identical queries.

The unpooled control gives random k3 risk 0.64% with a lower life limit, whereas the hierarchical value is 13.19%. The physical Weibull random-fatigue-limit baseline used by the external paper has not been reproduced. Hence these experiments do not establish best available fatigue performance. The curved model's random k3 risk is essentially unchanged at 13.20%; the twenty-source-bootstrap mixture has 16.42%, with all twenty saved replicates converging. This finite sensitivity is not a confidence or tolerance bound, and mixing population uncertainty does not automatically make a pointwise decile conservative. Chronological bootstrap k3 remains 1.96%, still only one campaign. External paired-contrast confidence fields are correctly blank rather than pretending that one campaign supports between-campaign uncertainty estimation.

## Provenance: corrections to our benchmark, not accusations

The phase-2 audit establishes that DOI membership and curve IDs are insufficient to define independent specimens, batches or new conditions. Condition IDs usually identify plotted S–N curves. Wood explicitly has seven groups from three builds. Publication-held-out evaluation avoids direct same-publication training leakage, but does not magically certify unseen-build or unseen-laboratory performance.

Merot DOI 10.1016/j.ijfatigue.2021.106552 is an explicit step-by-step fatigue protocol: 10^6 cycles per step, +25 MPa stress increments, 63 physical specimens versus 98 database plot rows. Empty step markers and damage-equivalent calculated reference stresses cannot be treated as 98 independent constant-amplitude failure/right-censored lifetimes. Whole-source exclusion corrects **our v1 eligibility assumption**. Wood a0554_d006_p004 was a failure after the extended test, not a runout, and Renishaw a0577_d003 lacks the EOS heat treatment incorrectly copied into the database. These are specific corrections backed by primary methods/figures, not grounds to discredit unrelated original papers.

The local original-Centola map `data/processed/centola_source_overlap.csv` contains seventeen sources and does **not** include Merot 106552, Wood ma12244203 or Kędziora ma15186278. Consequently these three corrections cannot be advertised as demonstrated errors in Centola's unavailable 518-row data. Several source DOIs overlap, but the original rows/code were never obtained; there is no exact-row reproduction or verified defect in their model.

Zhang's machine-learning paper 10.1016/j.ijfatigue.2019.105194 explicitly states reuse of data from prior papers, with references resolving to three of the related primary DOI series. That is publication-level reuse, ordinarily legitimate when cited; it requires grouping for independent evaluation, not an accusation of misconduct. Other strong near-coordinate families retain uncertainty about specimen identity. The Cui link has primary-source campaign evidence from the separate audit and is grouped in the final gate. Do not upgrade numerical coordinate correspondence to proven specimen duplication.

The retained main gate is source-method checked with several point/axis checks, not a complete new redigitization or a verified specimen ledger. Afkhami's constant-amplitude methods were checked but its Figure 19/21 stress-axis convention remains visually unresolved; the explicit exclusion sensitivity is therefore essential. The report should say “provenance-audited with recorded unresolved items”, not “all raw data fully verified”.

## What is worth presenting to Tridello

A defensible statement is: “After auditing an open literature corpus and holding out sources, three endpoint observations often corrected transferred fatigue curves, but finite-source calibration was vacuous, source-level risk was heterogeneous, and an external campaign exposed large initial overprediction. We have reproducible evidence about where the few-shot lower-bound problem fails, and want to test a stronger physical/censor-aware approach on genuinely independent campaigns.” This invites a technically useful discussion without presenting a negative primary result as success.

The previous v1 outreach brief should be explicitly superseded by a correction addendum before any reuse. Preserve its numerical receipts but state that its constant-amplitude eligibility premise was imperfect. Do not imply that the Merot issue affected Centola or that the new benchmark reproduced their method. No correspondence has been sent as part of this review.

## Precise next technical direction

1. Define the target before adding models: a new verified build/process condition at a stated stress and load ratio, a single consistent physical endpoint, and a specified test budget including planned censoring stops. Obtain specimen/build identifiers, source-figure links, load histories, failed-specimen censoring plans where available, and raw original rows. More plot points from the same campaign do not solve the source-calibration deficit.
2. Establish a genuine physical comparator: reproduce the external paper's random-fatigue-limit model, with a censored monotone Basquin and unpooled shift as controls. Compare a limited model that allows source/condition slope or fatigue-limit heterogeneity and retains population-parameter uncertainty. An intercept-only update cannot represent every new condition, while a two-slope curve did not repair the observed transfer risk. Select among these using training sources only; avoid tuning on Trombley or Lai queries after these outcomes.
3. Separate two estimands: few tests improving median/decile curve fit, and few tests supporting a lower-life bound with explicit coverage. For the latter collect enough genuinely independent calibration episodes to support the intended rank, plus uncertainty precision beyond the bare nine-source minimum. Coverage over a random source mixture must not be described as every-condition safety or R90C90 component certification. If the corpus cannot support the target, report that bound as unavailable rather than relax the rank.
4. Use a preplanned sequential test study with the same held-query population for every support policy. Choose stress levels to learn both the relevant life region and the suspected knee, count runouts in the budget, and retain likelihood-based censor handling. Compare fixed balanced-stress, random and model-guided allocation under identical campaign/stress/query targets. A favorable historical chronological split alone does not validate an experimental allocation rule.
5. Freeze the success rule before new data: at a common risk target, require a materially larger lower-life limit, paired intervals excluding trivial gain, no unacceptable identified risk increase, and acceptable worst-source/condition behavior. Reserve new campaigns for final assessment. Treat the present campaign as development evidence after this review, not as an indefinitely reusable untouched test set.

The highest-value next contribution is therefore **a physically meaningful, censor-aware transfer benchmark plus an experimentally testable lower-bound method that knows when the few-test budget is insufficient**. Merely adding a larger neural network or reporting the favorable source average would not close the demonstrated gap.
