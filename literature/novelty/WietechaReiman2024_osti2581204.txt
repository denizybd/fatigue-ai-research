Abstract  11 
The development of robust fatigue property data set s for additively manufactured 316L austenitic stain less 12 
steels is hindered by the small sample sizes, incon sistent reporting of material properties and proces sing conditions, 13 
and differences in testing procedures for current s ources of testing data.  By applying standardizatio n and 14 
categorization protocols to account for these short comings, historical data sets were aggregated into a single large 15 
data set and analyzed in greater detail, providing new levels of insight into the stress-controlled fa tigue behavior of 16 
additively manufactured materials.  Using this sing le aggregated data set, scatter was quantified usin g an eigenvalue 17 
analysis, and a multi-variable statistical model fo r predicting the mean fatigue life was developed.  Interactions 18 
between variables and the sensitivity to different loading conditions were then identified, with the s catter in the data 19 
being attributed to differences in testing and post -processing condition such as surface condition and  heat treatment, 20 
and the model was used to identify the role that ea ch variable plays in determining fatigue life.  An analysis of the 21 
fatigue responses across the aggregated data set us ing clustering algorithms allowed two distinct tren ds to be 22 
identified and attributed to the differing roles of  microstructure and porosity on the resulting fatig ue lives across 23 
different equivalent stress amplitude ranges.   24

1.  Introduction 30 
Approximately 90 % of metallic component and struct ural failures are attributed to fatigue mechanisms [1], but 31 
the scatter inherent in fatigue testing data introd uces significant challenges for the identification of suitable design 32 
criteria.  In cast and wrought materials, extensive  datasets [2] have been produced under highly contr olled material, 33 
loading, and environmental conditions [3] to develo p appropriate design standards for fatigue critical  applications.  34 
Compositional variations driven by heat-to-heat var iability or small changes in processing conditions can also 35 
contribute to scatter and are typically mitigated t hrough highly controlled post process heat treatmen ts [4] or 36 
thermomechanical processing [5].   37 
While fracture-mechanics approaches [6] are availab le for identifying design allowable values for fati gue 38 
critical applications, their wide-spread use is lim ited by a dearth of high-fidelity material property  data.  In their 39 
place, design standards for pressure vessels [7], b ridge components [8], and aerospace applications [9 ] employ 40 
penalties, safety factors, or fatigue-strength redu ction factors to the applied strains or stresses [2 ].  Fatigue design 41 
curves [10] are then constructed to account for the  effects of scatter in fatigue life originating fro m differences in 42 
surface roughness [11], tensile residual stresses [ 6], and, recently, for processing routes, such as a dditive 43 
manufacturing (AM) [12]. 44 
Development of these safety factors for AM material s, however, is limited, in part, by the impact of t he 45 
complex processing routes inherent in the different  AM processes.  For example, laser-based powder bed  fusion (L-46 
PBF) AM processes [13,14] are characterized by rapi d solidification conditions that produce microstruc tures 47 
dominated by dendritic or cellular structures [15] and complex dislocation substructures [16].  These materials are 48 
also susceptible to the formation of porosity that primarily originates from lack of fusion, gas poros ity, and keyhole 49

The variability in the available stress-controlled fatigue data for AM processed 316L austenitic stain less steel 75 
has been exacerbated by the small sample sizes and little replication within individual data sets and limited the 76 
ability to determine how fatigue properties are imp acted by AM processes.  While interest is growing i n being able 77 
to compile such historical data [46], strategies to  manipulate and model the data are currently lackin g.  When the 78 
data sets were aggregated, a relatively large amoun t of data became available, but analysis of this la rger data set in a 79 
unified manner required a more in-depth statistical  analysis.  In this analysis, categorization and st andardization 80 
protocols were applied to individual data sets to a ccount for differences in testing procedures, mater ial properties, 81 
and surface conditions.  After applying these stand ardization protocols, the data were aggregated, and  statistical 82 
analysis tools were then used to identify sources o f scatter and interactions between different variab le categories that 83 
contribute to fatigue life.  Clustering algorithms were then applied to the calculated fatigue respons es in the 84 
available data, allowing trends originating from di fferences in the microstructure- and porosity-drive n mechanisms 85 
for fatigue failure to be identified.   86

causing these pores to have a larger radius of curv ature along the loading axis and lowering the stres s concentration.  247

4. Summary and Conclusions  459 
Stress-controlled fatigue data for AM fabricated 31 6L austenitic stainless steels are characterized by  small 460 
sample sizes and a wide range of testing conditions  which limit broader analysis of fatigue performanc e and the 461 
development of robust and representative mechanical  properties.  Individual data sets were first stand ardized to 462 
account for differences in testing configuration an d categorized to identify differences in material p roperties, 463 
processing, and surface conditions and aggregated i nto a single large data set.  A statistical methodo logy was then 464 
developed and tested to identify larger trends and discern mechanisms contributing to fatigue failure.    Sources of 465 
scatter in the larger data set and interactions bet ween the different categorized variables and their respective effects 466 
on the resulting fatigue lives were identified.  Ex panding this analysis to the use of clustering algo rithms to identify 467 

=== PDF PAGE 28 ===
28 
 
trends in the fatigue response allowed for the iden tification of microstructural- and porosity-driven mechanisms that 468 
controlled fatigue behavior.  Additional conclusions are provided below in more detail.   469 
• A methodology for standardizing, categorizing, and clustering available fatigue data was developed to 470 
allow for the statistical analysis of a single aggr egated data set obtained from available smaller 471 
disconnected data sets.   472 
• Scatter in the fatigue life data of AM fabricated 316L austenitic stainless steel was quantified by calculating 473 
eigenvalues from the covariance between experimenta l fatigue life and the equivalent stress amplitude.   474 
Based on these calculations, lower levels of scatte r in the data were identified and corresponded with  lower 475 
load ratios, rotating-bending testing conditions, a nd machined surfaces. Scatter from the as-deposited  476 
condition ( eigenvalue  = 1.647) was higher than under stress relief (1.52 8) and annealed (1.488) conditions 477 
with the greatest reduction of scatter found after hot-isostatic pressing (1.242).  478 
• A statistical model was formulated and used to quan tify contributions to fatigue life from the materia l 479 
properties, surface condition, and post-process hea t treatments.  While the model only demonstrated a 480 
moderate fit with an adjusted correlation coefficie nt ( R2adjusted ) of 0.645, it proved superior to direct 481 
comparisons of the mean fatigue lives for these var iables and allowed the impact of each variable on 482 
fatigue life to be quantified.  For example, mean f atigue life increased by about 1.5 orders of magnit ude 483 
more than that observed in the as deposited and stress relieved conditions, with the application of HIP. 484 
• Statistical clustering of the aggregated fatigue re sponse data allowed two groups of fatigue responses  485 
characterized by shallow ( N
 = -0.0830 ± 0.0292) and steep ( N
 = -0.2390 ± 0.0542) slopes to be identified 486 
with a classification accuracy of 82.9 %.  After ap plying a RPARTS analysis to the data sets, the shal low 487 
fatigue response group was characterized by moderat e ductility, application of HIP, and possessing a 488 
machined surface.  The steep fatigue response group  contained samples with differing levels of process ing 489 
porosity that were not healed by post-process HIP treatments or machining of the as deposited surfaces.   490 
• By comparing the shallow and steep fatigue response  groups for the AM fabricated materials with wrough t 491 
data, similarities between microstructure and notch ed behavior in the wrought material were identified  492 
(confidence level of 99 %, α = 0.001).  AM fabricated materials with a shallow fatigue response displayed 493 
low levels of scatter ( eigenvalue  = 1.566) and similar fatigue lives to unnotched wr ought materials.  On the 494
