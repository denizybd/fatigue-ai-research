182979 - Multi-Fidelity Machine Learning With Uncertainty Quantification for Probabilistic Fatigue Crack Growth of Additively Manufactured Alloys
The fatigue crack growth of additively manufactured (AM) metals shows significant scatter due to uncertainties coming from process-induced defects, surface roughness, and microstructural variability. High-fidelity finite element method simulations can capture these complex micro-mechanisms but are computationally prohibitive for the number of evaluations required for robust uncertainty quantification (UQ). This work presents a physics-informed multi-fidelity deep learning (MF-PINN) method to achieve efficient and reliable UQ for the fatigue crack growth of AM metals.
First, our method combines a small set of high-fidelity FEM simulations or experiment data guided by a physics-informed neural network (PINN) with a large dataset of fast low-fidelity evaluations. The PINN is trained to approximate the fatigue damage accumulation law, incorporating known physical constraints. A multi-fidelity deep neural network learns the complex, non-linear correlation between low- and high-fidelity data, enabling the accurate and efficient fatigue life prediction.
Second, our method integrates conformal prediction (CP) on top of the multi-fidelity network. This technique generates provably valid prediction intervals for each fatigue life estimate, providing a distribution-free measure of uncertainty that is crucial for risk-aware design. Another strength of integrating CP in MF-PINN is that it can give you well-calibrated prediction intervals at any confidence level at low computational cost, which many other UQ methods struggle to provide.
We demonstrate the method's capability by generating probabilistic fatigue crack growth curves. We also demonstrate uncertainty propagation from critical inputs including defect size and applied stress amplitude to fatigue lives. This MF-PINN with UQ method provides a convenient and trustworthy tool for accelerating reliable anti-fatigue design of AM components under uncertainty
