# What the identity-qualified recovery establishes

The provenance objective has materially advanced. One additional room-temperature, axial force-controlled author campaign now has directly documented author specimen references, physical build IDs and powder-lot IDs. Its primary source table contains 43 nominal-stress first exposures across three physical builds. The acquisition benchmark uses 31 of those specimens in three conditions across two builds, because the other conditions are too small for the frozen query/pool rule. Twenty-one of the 31 are pre-corroded and ten are reference specimens. Both benchmarked builds share one powder lot, and all are one author campaign. The attainable budgets are k=3 and k=5; k=10 is not feasible.

Nine small-volume specimens have local Kt-adjusted stress and remain a separate sensitivity under the frozen primary protocol. An optional source-specific nominal conversion crosswalk was derived, but was not used in the primary fits or results. Eighteen saline/35C records remain outside the room-temperature air panel. The six-specimen Dastgerdi pilot has a source-backed common physical build but unstated temperature and remains a separate panel. This is not two fully qualified room-temperature campaigns, and the source recovery is retrospective rather than a new physical experiment.

Both cap runs pass independent identity, arithmetic and source-exclusion checks. Each has 50 tasks, 11,520 repeated prediction instances and 920 selected-test rows. All 1,728 absolute and 2,304 paired aggregate means were recomputed independently; maximum discrepancy is 9.31e-10 from cycle-scale floating-point arithmetic. The two RFL population fits converge without fallback or parameter-bound hits. This verifies numerical execution and provenance handling; it does not validate predictive coverage.

The source-matched 10^6 cap is the more directly relevant operational planning scenario: surviving initial blocks actually stop at 10^6 cycles. The 10^7 acquisition planning cap is deliberately misspecified while retaining those actual earlier right censors. Across the two runs, all 8,640 static-policy prediction instances and their observed support-cycle totals are exactly equal. Only an outcome-adaptive planning policy can change its selection in response to the different assumed cap; the larger planning cap does not create longer source tests.

At k=3 with the 10^6 cap, high-stress selection raises hierarchical Basquin lower-life utility by 0.125794 log10 decades versus random (about 34%) and RFL by 0.035352 decades (about 8.5%). Observed mean support cycles decrease from 2.306 million to 2.133 million, approximately 7.5%. That is a bounded cycle-cost result for this recovered cohort, not the roughly 70% reduction seen in different earlier campaigns, and not a wall-clock or manufacturing-cost saving.

The censoring uncertainty prevents a stronger reliability conclusion. Hierarchical Basquin's absolute risk identification interval at high-stress k=3 is about [5.0%,38.9%]; RFL's is [1.67%,9.17%]. These are finite-data identification ranges, not confidence intervals. For high stress versus random, the RFL paired true-risk-change identification range is approximately [-1.67,+6.67] percentage points; hierarchical Basquin is approximately [-4.44,+26.11] points. Thus increased life utility does not rule out a material risk increase. Sparse all-runout supports also leave the matched unpooled intercept unavailable in some conditions/splits; its availability must accompany its apparent risk.

Every panel in these runs contains one author campaign. Consequently the campaign-bootstrap confidence intervals are correctly unavailable. Zero automatically passed gates mean **not evaluable with the required sampling intervals**, rather than proof that every policy failed. Conversely, low observed violations, a favorable point identification upper bound, or 11,520 repeated prediction instances cannot establish a general 90% life-bound guarantee.

The defensible result is a stronger source-traced pilot and a useful test of model/selection/censoring interactions. A new generally dependable acquisition method is not established. To meet a two-campaign room-temperature validation requirement, at least one more independent campaign must have specimen/build mapping, compatible endpoints and enough distinct stress levels. Existing unresolved Diller/Tomaszewski records cannot be promoted merely because the current results are favorable. No correspondence is authorized or has been sent.
