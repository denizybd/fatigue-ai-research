# Phase-3 claim review after the final exploratory replay

The current evidence does not establish a new generally superior stress-acquisition policy. The observed benefit is model dependent. This is a substantive failure of the proposed broad contribution, rather than an integration or reproducibility failure.

On the covariate-defined MULTISTRESS subset of the two new specimen-identified campaigns, k=3 and the 10^7 cycle cap, high-stress selection versus random increases hierarchical Basquin q10 by 0.079069 log10 decades (about 20%). The corresponding RFL difference is -0.114591 decades (about 23% shorter lower-life bounds). The RFL risk also decreases: shorter bounds can be appropriately conservative, so this reversal is a utility–risk tradeoff and cannot alone label the policy harmful. It does show that one cannot infer a model-independent information gain from the hierarchical Basquin improvement.

The proposed active-query-variance policy also lacks general superiority. At this budget/cap, its hierarchical Basquin utility is -0.008635 decades versus random; RFL is -0.019482 decades with an interval spanning zero. Local-slope and curved-model behavior differs. The high-stress RFL utility reversal persists at the 10^6 cap (-0.123006 decades). Exact numbers are exploratory campaign-weighted contrasts from the frozen predictions, not fresh confirmatory experiments.

High-stress testing can therefore be reported as a candidate inexpensive operational heuristic for the reviewed campaigns and selected models. It is not a validated generally best policy. The observed support-cycle savings are useful pilot evidence, but they do not establish manufacturing-cost savings, wall-clock speedups or prospective acceptance.

The two new large campaigns lack verified specimen-to-build mappings. The six-specimen single-build pilot lacks an explicit test-temperature statement and has a precautionary source-family exclusion. Repeated seeds, many conditions, 180,960 prediction instances or multiple published figures do not add independent campaigns. Bootstrap limits with two campaigns describe this small empirical sample; passing a descriptive gate is insufficient for a general reliable-90%-bound claim. Normal-normal RFL is a stronger physical comparator, but it is not the published Trombley Weibull replica and still omits population parameter uncertainty.

A defensible strong next study needs genuinely independent, specimen/build-traced campaigns with compatible endpoint/censor definitions, a prospectively frozen acquisition comparison, and model-robust utility conditional on an acceptable risk bound. Without those data, the present package supports a reproducible methodological pilot and a model-dependence/negative result, rather than a novel dependable-design method.
