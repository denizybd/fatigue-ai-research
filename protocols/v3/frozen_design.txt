PHASE 3: COMMON-QUERY STRESS ACQUISITION, EXPLORATORY REPLAY
Recorded before phase-3 model fitting or prediction; public source outcomes and
phase-2 results have already been read. This is not a blinded prospective trial.

Question: With the same held-out physical specimens, do 3/5/10 selected tests
improve 10th-percentile life predictions, observed conservative-bound risk,
availability and cycle cost over random selection and strong censored models?

Source gate: require author specimen IDs and a source-supported specimen-to-build
or production-batch mapping for the identity-qualified external panel. A paper
explicitly stating one build is acceptable as a source-backed canonical mapping,
and must be distinguished from an author-assigned build ID. Keep missing batch
identity, ambiguous stress units, interrupted tests, reused specimens and uncertain
physical overlap in separate panels. Do not treat database curve IDs as builds.
Primary scope: AM316L LPBF, room-temperature axial constant-amplitude force tests.
Different frequency/endpoints are displayed and are not exchangeability evidence.

Data: historical 309-point/11-source phase-2 strict cohort is training population.
Historical leave-one-source-out replay is explicitly reused exploratory evidence.
Trombley is one previously examined external campaign, not a fresh confirmation.
New identity-qualified campaigns are held out of all population fitting. Small
campaigns are retained as pilots rather than excluded by a manufactured n>=15 rule.
All campaign reuse relations must be audited before labeling independent data.

For each condition and seeds 0..9, SHA256-sort specimen IDs with seed/condition.
Fix query IDs first: n_query=max(3,floor(.4*n)). All remaining specimens form the
acquisition pool; all policies/models share the identical query set and pool.
Budget k=0,3,5,10 only if at least that many acquisition candidates exist.
Conditions with n<6 do not enter acquisition comparisons. Two batches of the same
campaign are retained separately but are NOT independent campaigns for uncertainty.
Primary per-test administrative cap C=10^7 cycles; sensitivity C=10^6. Support
endpoints are truncated at C. Earlier author runouts remain right-censored at their
actual endpoint. Expected-information planning uses C, not an unrevealed endpoint.
Queries preserve original censoring. C is a replay intervention, not a new test.

Acquisition policies (nested prefixes; ties broken by outcome-blind SHA256 ranks):
1 random: hash order of pool.
2 stress_span: highest stress, lowest stress, then farthest log-stress from selected
  stresses. This covers the observed candidate stress range; no candidate y/event.
3 high_stress: decreasing stress. Cheap-failure control, not a presumed optimum.
4 active_query_variance: sequential expected Fisher information reduction of
  mean prediction variance on the known query stress covariates, using a target
  intercept/slope Gaussian prior and censored lognormal straight Basquin likelihood.
  Prior mean/fixed scatter/intercept SD come from historical hierarchical fit;
  slope SD=max(1,abs(training slope)/2), fixed in advance. Current target MAP and
  observed Hessian use selected supports only. For candidate x=(1,logS-Scenter),
  z=(logC-mu)/sigma, expected location information is
  [Phi(z)-z*phi(z)+phi(z)^2/Phi(-z)]/sigma^2.
  Score = I*(x' V Q V x)/(1+I*x' V x), Q=mean(x_query*x_query').
  No unknown candidate endpoint, query endpoint, or candidate runout flag is used.
  This policy and its design criterion are existing statistical design ideas;
  implementing a policy is not a novelty claim.

Prediction models, all fitted with proper right-censor likelihood:
pooled lognormal monotone Basquin; target-only intercept MLE sharing training
hierarchical slope/scatter (unavailable if all supports run out); hierarchical
censored Basquin; hierarchical continuous two-slope Basquin with train median knot;
normal-normal random-fatigue-limit model (finite failures plus mass at infinity),
with a target life-shift prior SD fixed to training hierarchical tau; and the
intercept/slope Gaussian-prior local MAP/Laplace approximation used by acquisition.
Model population parameter uncertainty is omitted and explicitly acknowledged.
RFL is not the exact published Weibull model; no exact reproduction claim.
No outcome-driven hyperparameter or policy selection. Failed/nonidentified fits
are reported, never silently replaced by another model. Infinite RFL lower bounds
count as available but unsafe whenever a finite query fails; their rate is shown.

Metrics at alpha=.10: known risk=mean(event*(logL>logY)), upper risk=mean(logL>logY),
so right-censored true risk lies between these. Include interval identification
limits for paired differences. Utility is mean log10 lower-life gain on common
finite available query pairs, with availability/positive infinity reported beside
it. Also report conservative log-life shortfall for observed failures, observed
support cycles, stress choices and all-runout support rate. Never infer benefit
from larger L alone. Per condition->campaign->seed averaging avoids treating all
specimens/seeds as independent campaigns. Paired campaign bootstrap 2000, seed231;
CI unavailable for one independent campaign, and weak with two or three campaigns.

Success gate: a policy must improve mean lower-life utility versus random while
its paired true-risk increase upper 95% endpoint is <=.02, and availability >=.95.
Absolute reliability also requires upper-risk upper 95% endpoint <=.10.
If data cannot support these gates, report insufficient evidence or failure, not
safe/guaranteed/useful generalization. Repeated query splits are sensitivity, not
additional independent experiments. No conformal population guarantee is claimed.

Sensitivities: same protocol with 10^6 cap; remove ambiguous/unverified batches;
separate ultrasonic Trombley; RFL quadrature convergence and bound diagnostics;
compare identical query IDs across policies; candidate-outcome perturbation test
must leave the next choice unchanged before revealing the candidate endpoint.
Hash this protocol, normalized inputs, split IDs, code and fit receipts in output.
