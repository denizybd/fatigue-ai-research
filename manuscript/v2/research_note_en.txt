1
Provenance first: how far do three fatigue
endpoints carry a new condition?
A source-audited, censored AM316L benchmark with an acquired external campaign. The prespecified useful-calibration
criterion was not met.
Finding
In a retrospective full-training model-based track, three endpoints per new condition increased the
geometric lower-life limit by 1.92x while the source-balanced danger identification interval fell from
13.35-15.41% to 8.31-8.93%. The descriptive source-bootstrap gain interval is 0.84-3.65x and includes no
improvement. Some sources became less safe. This is a promising aggregate trend, not a demonstrated
reliable-design algorithm.
Why provenance had to precede fitting
The original public-data pilot contained 1,126 approximately digitized points. Original-methods review
identified 98 records from Merot et al. as stepped-load/damage-equivalent observations rather than
ordinary independent constant-amplitude lifetimes. They were excluded. Further publication-level reuse,
unresolved figure-to-experiment mappings and a maximum-stress/amplitude conflict required quarantine.
The main cohort contains 309 points, 32 database curves and 11 source blocks with reviewed
experimental methods. These are not 11 certified independent laboratories, nor are database row IDs
physical specimen IDs.
Additional source-supported corrections: one Wood endpoint labelled runout was a fracture; one Kedziora Renishaw group
inherited an unsupported heat treatment. Corrections are applied only to derived phase-2 data. Raw files and v1 outputs
remain immutable. These findings concern our open-data benchmark and do not prove an error in Centola et al.’s unavailable
518-row dataset. [1-4]

Frozen question and models
For an unseen provenance source, do 3, 5 or 10 endpoints from one new condition improve useful

Conditions mean source S-N series, not verified build batches. All model features and population

Seeds 0, 17 and 42 define outcome-blind record-ID permutations. Reserve the maximum support pool
once per panel and use all remaining records as the same queries for every k. The panels have different
populations; they are not a single learning curve. Runouts contribute a survival likelihood, not an exact
failure density.
Baselines: (i) pooled monotone censored lognormal Basquin; (ii) a target-only intercept update with
historical slope/scatter; (iii) a condition-random-intercept Basquin model, updating a proper target
intercept posterior with censored support. The predictive lower decile integrates specimen scatter and
target intercept uncertainty. Population parameters are empirical Bayes estimates; the main bounds omit
their estimation uncertainty. A training-source bootstrap mixture with 20 replicates from 11 historical
sources assesses this externally. A continuous two-slope monotone curve with a training-only median
stress knot is a prespecified curvature sensitivity.
Calibration and claim discipline
The original primary criterion required a useful 90% endpoint-calibrated bound, risk at most 10%, a paired
risk-increase limit and a greater-than-1.25x life gain supported by descriptive source-bootstrap intervals.
Source review leaves only 4-6 calibration blocks when at least four fitting blocks are retained. The required
finite-sample rank exceeds the calibration count, so the literal bound is zero. It is safe but useless and fails
the utility criterion. This finite-rank result applies to this frozen calibration protocol, not to every
parametric or survival method.
A separately recorded full-training raw track fits all other verified sources and reports model-based 10th
percentiles. It does not replace the failed calibrated primary test. Hierarchical borrowing and modern
survival conformal prediction are established prior work. Missing potential censoring times on failures rule
out direct Type-I calibration, but modern right-censoring methods exist; none guarantees arbitrary
manufacturing-source shift. [5-8]
Risk and utility
Danger is L>T. For a runout C<T, L<=C establishes safety for that event; L>C remains ambiguous. Report
[known violations/n, (known+ambiguous)/n]. These are censoring identification endpoints, not confidence
intervals. Average within curve, then source, then seeds. Utility is paired log10(Lk/L0) on identical queries;
larger unsafe limits are not useful. Source bootstrap intervals are descriptive because outer models reuse
training sources.

3
Internal results: average gain is not source reliability
The full-training raw A-panel gain is 1.92x (0.84-3.65x descriptive 95% interval). The upper bootstrap
endpoint for the upper identified true-risk increase is +9.30 percentage points, above the 2-point
noninferiority margin. Hence even the secondary raw result does not meet the intended robust
positive-claim standard.
Solberg’s source risk upper endpoint falls from 100% to 3.03%, driving most of the mean improvement. Lai increases from
23.33% to 42.60%; Spierings from 0% to 6.94%; Kedziora from 0% to 11.11%. These are small-source descriptive estimates,
not certificates. Deleting the unfavorable sources or treating seed repeats as new experiments would be invalid.

4
Acquired external campaign: the limits become shorter and less dangerous
Trombley, Birnbaum and Allison’s public Materials Commons pages provide 129 complete named as-built
specimens: 44/40/45 at 1.5/2.5/5.0 mm diameter. The article states 130; one incomplete page is excluded
without imputation. All 154 available fatigue records and the 129-record subset were independently
checked against the public HTML. No strong historical-coordinate reuse signal was found. This is one
campaign with three related conditions. Four builds are reported, but specimen-to-build mapping is not
certified. [9-10]
Tests were performed in 2019-2021, at 20 kHz and R=-1. The failure endpoint is a
resonance-drop/visible-crack criterion, potentially before full fracture. Thus this is a cross-campaign,
cross-endpoint stress test, not clean validation of an identical fracture-time distribution. Preserve actual
runout endpoints; do not invent a universal potential censoring time for failures.

Random k3 reduces danger from 53.66% to 13.19%, still above 10%, by lowering the life limit about 4.68x
rather than extending it. The 20-population-bootstrap mixture has 16.42% random-k3 danger; the
two-slope model has 13.20%. Chronological k3 gives 1.96% on its own query population. We retain every
result rather than select the most favorable mechanism.
The original article uses a Weibull random-fatigue-limit model. Our finite curvature control is not its reproduction, and
superiority over that relevant physical comparator is untested. This limitation must remain in any communication. [9,11]

5
Controls, sensitivities and verification
An explicitly post-result diagnostic holds the hierarchical population slope/scatter fixed and replaces prior
borrowing with an unpooled censored target intercept. At k0 its reference equals the same hierarchical
predictive distribution; all-runout supports remain unavailable. This control avoids attributing a changed
population scatter to a benefit from shrinkage. It was not used to rescue the original primary claim.
On matched internal k3 queries, hierarchical versus this simple control gives about 1.10x life, with a
descriptive interval including no gain. Externally on random supports, the control has 6.99% danger versus
13.19% for the hierarchy, at approximately 1/1.42 of its life limit. There is a tradeoff, not demonstrated
prior-specific superiority. Availability and common-query masks are retained.
Near-coordinate clustering removes one historical point; the A-panel risk endpoints remain unchanged.
Excluding Afkhami, whose stress-axis visual verification remains unresolved, gives k3 risk 9.00-9.70%.
Full-family/weak-edge and broader database-labelled analyses are sensitivity results, not replacements for
reviewed-source evidence. They retain unresolved original-experiment and stress-definition issues.
All 31 synthetic model/evaluation tests pass. Two independently written arithmetic checks reproduce core
source-balanced summaries and paired censoring contrasts. Direct scalar numerical integration checks
mixed-censor posteriors. Critical strict, full-training and external predictions were replayed byte for byte.
These establish computational consistency, not independent physical replication. Fit statuses, split IDs,
hashes and exact commands are saved.
Boundaries
The cohort remains digitized with incomplete physical specimen/batch IDs. Several method-reviewed
sources lack exhaustive point matching. Original author endpoints and sampling protocols differ;
staircase-selected stresses and retrospective random supports are not an optimized sequential test policy.
Counts of endpoints are not measured machine hours or monetary savings. We did not run new physical
experiments, obtain Centola’s original data/code, reproduce the BNN/Owen calculations, or establish
general source-conditional coverage.

6
Contribution assessment and the next discriminating experiment
The defensible contribution is a provenance-corrected benchmark, concrete source/label corrections and a
documented counterexample to interpreting average few-shot improvement as reliable performance in
every new condition. The hierarchical method itself is established. This package supports a focused
technical discussion with Tridello, not a claim of a new validated fatigue-design algorithm.
The next question should be: with a fixed test budget, which stress levels reveal a harmful new-condition
shift and produce the largest useful lower-life limit at acceptable source-specific risk? First secure physical
specimen/build IDs, consistent failure definitions and actual censoring plans. Then benchmark the
published random-fatigue-limit model and the matched simple intercept control. Freeze one common
query set across random, stress-spanning and chronological support policies; choose any policy only using
development campaigns. Test on additional independent campaigns and report source-specific failures,
not only an average. A new campaign and a frozen protocol are required for a strong confirmatory claim.
